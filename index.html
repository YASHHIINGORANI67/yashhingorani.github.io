06 / MODEL EXPLORER

Research methodology explorer

Expand each method to learn how it is used in financial risk analysis.

Value at Risk (VaR)

VaR estimates a loss threshold for a specified confidence level and time horizon. For example, a one-day 99% VaR is a threshold exceeded on approximately 1% of days under the model's assumptions.

Expected Shortfall (ES)

Expected Shortfall estimates the average loss in the tail beyond a specified VaR threshold, subject to the chosen distribution and estimation method.

ARMA Models

Autoregressive Moving Average models describe linear dependence in stationary time series using past observations and past innovations.

GARCH Models

Generalized Autoregressive Conditional Heteroskedasticity models describe changing conditional variance, including volatility clustering.

Monte Carlo Simulation

Monte Carlo methods generate many simulated outcomes from a specified probabilistic model to approximate distributions and risk measures.

Extreme Value Theory

EVT provides statistical tools for modelling unusually large observations and distribution tails, with important threshold and sample-size assumptions.

Logistic Regression

Logistic regression models the probability of a binary event, such as borrower default, as a function of explanatory variables.

Ordinary Least Squares

OLS estimates linear regression coefficients by minimizing the sum of squared residuals. Inference depends on the relevant modelling assumptions.

Maximum Drawdown

Maximum drawdown measures the largest observed peak-to-trough percentage decline in a portfolio or asset value series.

MACD Strategy Analysis

Moving Average Convergence Divergence compares exponential moving averages to generate momentum-related signals. Backtests require careful treatment of transaction costs and overfitting.
